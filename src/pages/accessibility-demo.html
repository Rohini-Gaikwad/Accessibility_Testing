Accessible page structure

A well-structured page improves navigation, supports assistive technologies, and helps all users understand the content more easily.

Overview

Page structure provides meaning to content through headings, landmarks, labels, and grouping. This helps people who use keyboards, screen readers, and other assistive tools understand the layout and move through it efficiently.

  • Use semantic elements such as <header>, <nav>, and <main>.
  • Organise content with clear headings from h1 to h6.
  • Group related content with sections and articles.
  • Provide visible focus styles and a logical reading order.

Guidelines

The WCAG and WAI recommendations emphasise perceivable, operable, understandable, and robust content. Good structure supports these goals.

  • Include a clear page title and unique main heading.
  • Use landmarks to identify site regions and navigation.
  • Ensure each form control has an associated label.
  • Keep content and interaction patterns predictable.

Accessibility checklist

  • Keyboard users can reach all links and buttons.
  • Focus remains visible throughout the page.
  • Headings follow a logical hierarchy.
  • Page regions are announced correctly by screen readers.

Quick notes

Accessibility should be considered during design, development, and testing.

Common techniques

  • Use semantic HTML before custom widgets.
  • Provide strong contrast between text and background.
  • Do not rely on colour alone to convey meaning.
